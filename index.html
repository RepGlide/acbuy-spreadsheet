Four Identity Markers

Check Each Marker Separately

Before treating a page or link as one source, read each marker on its own — none of them confirms another.

Azure

Visible name

The name the page currently displays. What it shows, not what it is.

Vermilion

Exact domain

The real domain, subdomain, and path — read precisely, not from memory.

Lime

Page context

The title and surrounding content that say what the page is.

Silver

Final destination

Where a link or redirect actually lands after you follow it.

Relationship unknown

Four matching markers are still only an observation. They cannot automatically prove ownership or an official relationship.

Similar Names Are Not Evidence

A Look-Alike Is Not an Affiliation

Similar abbreviations, extended names, domains, and titles are not proof of a relationship.

1What a match can tell you

  • The two pages look alike.
  • They may share a name or a domain pattern.
  • A starting point for checking further.

2What a match cannot tell you

  • That the two are the same source.
  • That one owns, runs, or endorses the other.
  • Any official or verified relationship.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an ACBuy spreadsheet?

An ACBuy spreadsheet is a shared document that compiles listing references, category groupings, and product links for use with agent shopping services. It is a pointer to browse and re-check, not an official, live, or complete product catalog.

Is this website the official ACBuy service?

No. This website is an independent informational and navigation resource. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by ACBuy or Google.

What do the four identity markers mean?

Visible name is what a page currently displays. Exact domain is the real domain, subdomain, and path. Page context is what the title and surrounding content say the page is. Final destination is where a link or redirect actually lands. Each is observed separately.

Do matching names prove two pages are the same source?

No. Four matching markers are only an observation, not proof of ownership or an official relationship. Similar abbreviations, extended names, domains, or titles are not evidence of affiliation on their own.

What should I do when names or domains look similar?

Mark the relationship as unknown. Do not infer an official link from spelling similarity. Stop the inference when the evidence is not enough, and treat the two as separate until they are actually confirmed.

Where do I confirm a link belongs to the same source?

On the destination itself: read the visible name, the exact domain and path, and the page context of the page you actually landed on. The spreadsheet is a starting pointer, not proof of identity.
